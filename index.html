      About

      Background and appointments.

      Ph.D. in Marketing Kyungpook National University, 2019

      Joonheui Bae is a Professor of International Business and Trade in the Division of Interdisciplinary Studies for International Students at Sunmoon University, Korea. Bae received a Ph.D. in Marketing from Kyungpook National University in 2019.

      Bae's research asks how consumers decide in settings that technology keeps reshaping: virtual stores with varying levels of realism, recommendations and advice from generative AI, apologies from service robots, and peer-to-peer platforms where quality is hard to judge. A parallel stream examines how luxury, fashion, and sustainability messages persuade, and how K-culture shapes fashion consumption worldwide. Most of this work relies on controlled experiments, complemented by causal inference methods for field data. Bae leads CODE Lab (Consumer Decisions in Emerging Technologies).

      Before joining Sunmoon University in 2026, Bae was an Assistant Professor at The Hong Kong Polytechnic University (2023–2026), where Bae taught in the MA in Global Fashion Management and led fashion seminars in New York and Paris. Earlier appointments include Research Professor and Postdoctoral Fellow at Kyungpook National University and Senior Researcher at the Gyeongbuk Institute of IT Convergence Technology.

      The research has appeared in the Journal of Business Research, Journal of Retailing and Consumer Services, Computers in Human Behavior, International Journal of Advertising, and Internet Research, among others. Bae serves as Associate Editor of the Journal of Global Scholars of Marketing Science and sits on the editorial review boards of Internet Research and the Journal of Global Fashion Marketing.

      Research

      Where consumer psychology meets AI, virtual retail, and K-culture.

      CODE Lab runs experiments on how people choose when technology changes the setting: AI agents and service robots, virtual and phygital stores, and cultural content that travels across borders. The four themes below organize the lab's current projects. Students and collaborators interested in this work are welcome to get in touch.

      01

      Consumers, AI, and Robots

      When does a human-like AI help, and when does it backfire? This work covers AI speakers, recommendation agents, generative AI advisors, and how service robots should apologize after a failure.

      Related: Polite or playful? (CHB 2026); Human likeness and attachment effect on AI speakers (JBR 2022)
      02

      K-Culture and Fashion

      How does the global reach of Korean culture shape fashion and brand choices abroad? This newer stream connects cultural trends with consumer identity and cross-border branding.

      Related talk: K-Culture and Fashion: Recent Trends and Global Impact (2025 Global Art & Culture Conference)
      03

      Decisions on Digital Platforms

      How do the cues of a platform change what people choose? I study virtual and phygital retail, sharing and on-demand platforms, and nudges that steer users toward better conduct.

      Related: Less real, more distinctive (JRCS 2027); Lemons problem in collaborative consumption platforms (Internet Research 2018)
      04

      Fashion, Luxury, and Sustainability Communication

      What makes sustainability claims credible and luxury messages persuasive? Projects look at eco-labels, CSR and CSiR, influencer type, and luxury consumption in the metaverse.

      Related: Mindset meets endorser (JRCS 2026); Environmental cues and sustainable purchase (JBR 2020)

      Methods

      Online and lab experiments with process evidence (mediation, moderation), A/B tests on live platforms, and causal inference methods for field data.
